#Reflective Conversation with Materials

While reading this interview I noticed they introduced many subjects in a really short time, subjects such as: the flow state, trial and error, "Happy accidents", watch and learn, etc. I would say that with the summary of all of these, the main subject was about the process of making.

I read this interview through the perspective of an artist, and I felt related with many parts of the text, mostly the ones at the beginning. The way they talk about the jazz musicians with their improvisation, and the architect with her sketches and modifications, reminded me to the times were people enter to the Flow state. The state where we enter into a deep focus and total immersion into an activity, where awareness merge and time disappears. This used to happen to me a lot in cegep, whenever I was making an sculpture or a painting. It happened differently when I enter to university, my flow state would start after I learned the basics of coding, but would immediatly stop after having to debbug something. It wasn't as constant as before.

I also liked the part about the Back Talk. When we make judgements of our projects during the process, artists talk to its art and to themselves while creating. In the interview they say is not the same as Feedback, but I feel there's some similarity, becuase is like testing without asking and just taking note of the actions.

After the first half of the interview, the only part I liked the most was when they are talking about the studies of the two programs of Project Athena: the McCavity,made to be an intelligent tutor, and the Growltiger, considered a design tool. It was really interesting the way students found the second program to be more useful as something to learn from, than the one that was created to teach. In my opinion, I feel it's because most people retain information more easily with the "trial and error" rather than with "watch and learn".

This reading made me feel related with the journey artists take while creating and designing, and it also reminded me that we all perceive things differently.
